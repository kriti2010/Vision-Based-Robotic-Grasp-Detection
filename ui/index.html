The Negative Result on Sample Dispersion

A central empirical finding of the research paper: Sample dispersion does NOT improve failure prediction over calibrated scoring alone.

Core Thesis & Empirical Proof

While intuitive hypotheses suggest that the geometric dispersion of diffusion samples (e.g., DBSCAN cluster count, mode entropy, geodesic spread) indicates epistemic ambiguity and predicts physical grasp failure, our rigorous multi-view benchmark proves this hypothesis false. Calibrated confidence scoring alone achieves 0.665 AUROC, strictly outperforming dispersion-augmented models (0.640 AUROC) and cross-view features (0.479 AUROC).

Why Does Sample Dispersion Fail?

1. Diffusion Model Expressivity: The variance-exploding diffusion model with 12 DDIM steps already concentrates samples into valid local geometric modes. Residual dispersion across samples primarily reflects multi-modal antipodal symmetries of the object geometry rather than physical instability.

2. Information Redundancy: The PointNet-based success scorer with local grasp-frame coordinates already encodes local geometric curvature, finger collisions, and surface normals. Sample spread offers no orthogonal signal.

Practical Engineering Takeaway

Robotics practitioners can safely discard complex sample clustering and dispersion estimators in active grasping pipelines. A single well-calibrated scalar success scorer $s^*$ paired with conformal thresholding $\hat{\lambda}$ provides both superior discrimination and formal statistical risk bounds with minimal compute overhead.

Mathematical Formulations & Statistical Theory

The theoretical guarantees, Lie algebra representations, and risk bounds behind the project.

1. SE(3) Lie Group & Diffusion Reverse Process

Each 6-DoF grasp candidate consists of rotation $R \in SO(3)$, translation $t \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and opening width $w \in \mathbb{R}^+$:

Perturbation kernel: p_{0t}(g_t | g_0)
t_t = t_0 + σ_t * ε_t,   ε_t ~ N(0, I)
R_t = R_0 * Exp(σ_t * ω_t),  ω_t ~ N(0, I)

DDIM deterministic reverse step:
g_{t-1} = g_t - (σ_t - σ_{t-1}) * S_θ(g_t, t, P)

Rotations are represented continuously via 6D Gram-Schmidt projection or Lie algebra exponential map.

2. Calibrated Success Scoring & Temperature Scaling

The discriminator predicts grasp success logit $z(g, P)$ conditioned on global and local point cloud features:

Calibrated probability:
p(g, P) = σ(z(g, P) / T_opt)

Optimization:
T_opt = argmin_T - ∑ [y_i log σ(z_i / T) + (1 - y_i) log(1 - σ(z_i / T))]

Estimated Risk:
ρ = 1 - max_{k} s_k

3. Conformal Risk-Controlled Execution

Given risk tolerance $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, choose execution threshold $\hat{\lambda}$ on calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{calib}}$:

R̂(λ) = (1 / n) ∑_{i=1}^n Loss(Policy_λ(X_i), Y_i)

λ̂ = sup { λ ∈ [0, 1] : (n / (n + 1)) * R̂(λ) + (1 / (n + 1)) ≤ α }

Statistical Guarantee:
E[Loss(Policy_{λ̂})] ≤ α

Policy executes at first view where $\rho \le \hat{\lambda}$; if not and views remain, acquires view $t+1$; if views exhausted, abstains (Loss = 0).

4. Learn-then-Test Binomial Tail Bound

To control the probability of exceeding risk tolerance with finite samples:

p_val(λ) = BinomialTail(n, ∑ Loss_i(λ), α)

Fixed-sequence testing ensures:
P(R(Policy) > α) ≤ δ  (e.g., δ = 0.10 or 0.05)

Table 4 confirms this bound: empirical violation rate is well below the target failure probability.
